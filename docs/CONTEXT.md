# CONTEXT.md — Padaraksha Domain Glossary & Business Context

This file explains the business domain, terminology, and real-world workflows for the Padaraksha factory management system. Read this alongside CLAUDE.md to understand *why* the system works the way it does.

---

## What is Padaraksha?

Padaraksha (meaning "footwear protector" in Sanskrit) is a factory management system for a **job-work sandal manufacturing business**. The factory does not sell directly to consumers — it is a **contract manufacturer**. Provider companies send raw materials, the factory produces finished sandals, and sends them back.

---

## The Business Model (Job Work)

```
Provider Company
  → Sends raw materials + Purchase Order to Factory
  → Factory manufactures sandals
  → Factory sends finished goods back to Provider
  → Provider pays Factory for labour (not material)
```

The factory earns:
- A **labour charge** per piece produced
- A **5-day delivery incentive** (% of order value) for fast turnaround
- A **quality incentive** (% of order value) for low defect rate

The factory loses money when:
- Raw materials are **damaged** (deducted from receivables at PO price)
- Goods are **spoiled** and cannot be delivered
- Delivery is **late** (loses incentive)

## Glossary

### Article
A specific sandal design identified by an **Article Number** (e.g., "PR-204"). One article has many variants based on colour, size, gender, and left/right foot. The same article number may be produced for multiple provider companies. The Article definition includes its Bill of Materials and Work Process.

### Article Variant
A specific combination of: Article + Colour + Size + Gender (M/W/K/U) + Foot (L/R/Pair). Example: "PR-204 / Brown / Size 7 / Men / Pair". Variants are tracked separately in inward entries and production.

### BOM (Bill of Materials)
The list of raw materials required to produce **one piece** of an article. Example for PR-204: 1 insole, 1 pair lace, 1 upper, 2m thread, 10ml gum. BOM is identical for all variants of an article. BOM can be updated if the production process changes.

### Work Stage / Work Process
The ordered list of manufacturing steps required to complete an article. Example stages: Cutting → Upper Stitching → Sole Attachment → Finishing. Early stages may run in parallel; later stages are sequential. Each stage has a **pay rate per piece** for piece-rate workers. Stages are defined per article and can be updated mid-production.

### Purchase Order (PO)
A document generated by the **provider company's head office** and sent to the factory with a shipment of raw materials. Contains: PO number, date, article variants ordered, qty per variant, and reference price per raw material. The **5-day incentive clock starts on PO arrival date**.

### Department Dispatch Bill (Dept Bill)
A smaller bill sent by a **specific department** of the provider company (e.g., Insole Dept, Upper Dept) alongside their portion of raw materials. Each bill has its own bill number, is linked to the main PO, and shows what was dispatched and whether there is any shortage. Multiple dept bills can be linked to one PO. Bills may arrive same day as PO or a few days later.

### Inward Entry
The act of receiving and logging raw materials that arrive at the factory. One inward entry is created per PO receipt. It captures: actual qty received vs. expected qty, and all associated dept bills.

### Provider Shortage
When a department sends fewer raw materials than required (e.g., Insole Dept sends 95 instead of 100). This is the **provider's responsibility**, not the factory's. Logged against the dept bill. The provider is expected to send the remaining materials.

### Work Assignment
When a supervisor assigns a specific work stage to a worker for a specific article batch. Recorded as one entry even if covering many pieces (e.g., "Ramesh — Stitching — 50 pieces — Article PR-204"). Multiple workers can be assigned the same stage on the same batch.

### Rework
When a finished piece is rejected in QC (internal or by provider), it is sent back to the worker **whose stage caused the defect** for correction. Rework is **unpaid** — it is the worker fixing their own mistake. Tracked separately from original work. A piece may go through rework multiple times.

### Internal QC
Quality check performed by the factory's own QC staff or supervisor before outward delivery. Pieces are passed or failed. Failed pieces are assigned for rework. The QC process identifies which work stage caused the defect so the correct worker is held accountable.

### Outward Delivery
The shipment of finished sandals back to the provider company. May be partial (some pieces now, rest later). Records: qty dispatched, shortage (promised but not produced), spoiled (damaged beyond repair).

### Provider QC
The quality check performed by the provider company after they receive the goods. They may reject individual failed pieces or the entire shipment. Rejected goods are sent back to the factory for rework (still unpaid for the worker).

### Spoiled Piece
A sandal piece that was damaged during production and cannot be repaired. Counted as a factory loss. The cost of the raw materials is deducted from the factory's receivables at PO reference prices.

### Damage Repurchase
When the factory damages a raw material and must order a replacement from the provider. This is tracked separately from normal inward entries. Key metrics: order date, delivery date, days taken, pending items.

### Incentive (5-Day Delivery)
A percentage bonus earned on the order value if all pieces from a PO are delivered back to the provider within 5 days of the PO arrival date. Provider-side delays (late dept bills) do not disqualify the factory. Tracked per PO.

### Incentive (Quality)
A percentage bonus earned on the order value based on the quality of the finished goods. Exact rate TBD. Tracked per PO via a quality score field.

### Pay Types
- **Fixed**: Worker is paid a flat daily or monthly wage regardless of pieces done.
- **Piece Rate**: Worker is paid per piece completed per work stage.
- **Hybrid**: Fixed wage for the first 8 hours of the day; piece rate applies for work done beyond 8 hours.

### Attendance
Daily log of each worker's presence: Present / Absent / Half-Day. Used for fixed and hybrid payroll calculations.

### Payroll Run
A payroll calculation for a chosen date range (can be daily, weekly, or monthly — admin's choice). Aggregates: completed pieces (non-rework only), hours worked, deductions. Rework is shown separately but not paid.

## Real-World Workflow Walkthrough

### Day 1: PO Arrives
- Provider sends materials for Article PR-204, 200 pairs, with PO #1042.
- Three dept bills arrive: Insole Dept (200 units, full), Upper Dept (200 units, full), Thread Dept (190m — 10m short).
- Factory logs Inward Entry: PO #1042, all three dept bills, flags Thread Dept shortage.
- 5-day countdown begins. Deadline: Day 6.

### Day 2–3: Production
- Supervisor assigns Ramesh to Upper Stitching for 100 pieces.
- Supervisor assigns Suresh to Upper Stitching for 100 pieces.
- Supervisor assigns Ramesh to Sole Attachment for all 200 pieces.
- Workers complete tasks; supervisor logs completion with qty and time.

### Day 3: Internal QC
- QC checks 200 pieces. 190 pass. 10 fail (sole attachment defect).
- System identifies Ramesh did sole attachment → 10 pieces assigned to Ramesh for rework (unpaid).
- Ramesh fixes them. QC re-checks. All pass.

### Day 4: Outward Delivery
- Factory ships 198 pieces to provider (2 spoiled — logged as factory loss).
- Shortage of 2 noted. Thread shortage from provider (10m) noted as their liability.
- Delivery is within 5 days → factory is eligible for delivery incentive.

### Day 6: Provider QC Response
- Provider finds 5 more defects, sends them back.
- Factory logs provider return of 5 pieces.
- System identifies responsible worker → assigned for rework (unpaid).
- Reworked pieces reshipped.

### Month End: Payroll
- Admin runs payroll for the month.
- Ramesh: 300 pieces stitching (paid) + 100 pieces sole attachment (paid) + 15 pieces rework (unpaid, shown separately).
- Deductions: 1 day absent, advance of ₹500.
- Net pay calculated and shown.

## What the App Must Never Do

- Charge a worker for rework they didn't cause.
- Count rework pieces toward a worker's pay.
- Attribute provider shortages as factory losses.
- Mix data between different factory locations.
- Block production entry because a work stage sequence wasn't followed (no enforcement, only recording).
